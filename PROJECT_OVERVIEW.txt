WHAT IS THIS, IN ONE SENTENCE?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Flywheel is a piece of software that runs the "business machinery"
behind a paid newsletter — it finds things to write about, drafts the
newsletter, tests which ads bring in the cheapest subscribers, and
tracks whether the whole thing is making money — while a human stays
in charge of every risky decision (nothing sends, spends, or goes
live without a person clicking "approve").


THE BUSINESS THIS RUNS
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The newsletter is called "Hard Money Weekly" — a weekly email about
gold and bitcoin for people who already invest in one or both and
want a quick, no-hype summary of what moved the market and why.

The business model is simple in theory:
  1. Spend $1 on Facebook/Instagram ads to get someone to subscribe.
  2. That subscriber is worth more than $1 over time — through
     sponsors who pay to appear in the newsletter, affiliate deals,
     etc.
  3. If step 2 is reliably true, spend more on ads. If it isn't,
     stop spending and figure out why.

That loop — spend money, get a subscriber, make more money than you
spent, reinvest — is the "flywheel." The software's whole job is to
run that loop and tell a human operator (you) whether it's actually
spinning or not.


WHAT THE SOFTWARE ACTUALLY DOES (THE PIPELINE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Think of it as eight jobs, each one automated but supervised:

1. FIND CONTENT
   Every 6 hours, it scans news sites, Reddit, and Hacker News for
   articles relevant to gold/bitcoin macro news, and keeps only the
   good ones (an AI model scores relevance 0-100).

2. TRACK SUBSCRIBERS
   It talks to Beehiiv (the email platform that actually sends the
   newsletter and manages the subscriber list) to know who's
   subscribed, who unsubscribed, and — importantly — WHICH AD each
   subscriber came from, so we can tell which ads are worth the money.

3. WRITE A DRAFT
   On request, an AI model writes a full newsletter issue from the
   best recent content it found, plus 5 possible subject lines. A
   human reviews it on a dashboard and clicks "Approve," then "Send."
   The software never sends anything by itself.

4. THE APPROVAL DASHBOARD
   A simple webpage (only visible on your own computer right now)
   showing drafts to review, cash balance, and — once the business is
   running — ad performance and recommendations. This is the "cockpit."

5. MAKE AD CREATIVES
   For a given ad-testing "experiment" (e.g. "test 3 different
   angles for our free newsletter offer"), an AI model writes a batch
   of different ad variations (different hooks, different angles),
   checks they're not near-duplicates of each other, and uploads them
   to Facebook/Instagram — but always PAUSED. A human has to click
   "Activate" to make any ad actually spend money and go live.

6. CHECK THE RECEIPTS (ATTRIBUTION)
   Facebook will tell you "this ad got you 10 signups." This module
   double-checks that against what Beehiiv actually recorded, to
   catch cases where Facebook's number doesn't match reality.

7. CUT LOSSES AUTOMATICALLY (THE "REAPER")
   Every hour, it checks: has an ad spent way more than it should
   have with zero results? Has an experiment run past its deadline?
   If yes, it kills that ad/experiment automatically — pausing it on
   Facebook and marking it dead — no human needs to catch it, and
   there's no way to un-kill it (you start a new experiment instead).
   This is the safety net that stops money bleeding out unnoticed.

8. DECIDE WHERE TO SPEND NEXT (THE ALLOCATOR)
   This is the "brain" that looks at the real numbers — how much it
   costs to get a subscriber (CAC), how much a subscriber is worth
   over time (LTV), how fast the business earns back what it spent
   (payback period) — and recommends, per group of subscribers:
      - SCALE UP 20%    → this is working well, spend more
      - HOLD             → it's fine, don't change anything yet
      - CUT 50%          → this isn't working, spend less and try
                            something new
      - HALT ALL SPENDING → the business is close to running out of
                            cash — stop spending immediately
   It only RECOMMENDS. A human still has to click the button to
   actually raise a budget.


A FEW TERMS EXPLAINED SIMPLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------
CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)
    How much it costs, on average, to get one new subscriber.
    Example: spend $500 on ads, get 100 subscribers → CAC = $5.

LTV (Lifetime Value)
    How much money one subscriber is expected to bring in, total,
    before they eventually unsubscribe.

Payback period
    How many days it takes for a subscriber's value to earn back
    what it cost to acquire them. Under 30 days = great. Over 60
    days = probably not worth it.

Runway
    At the current rate of spending, how many days until the bank
    account hits zero. Below 21 days is treated as an emergency.

The "ledger"
    A permanent, unchangeable record of every dollar in and out
    (ad spend, sponsor payments, AI costs, etc.) — like a bank
    statement the software can never secretly edit or delete from.

"Approval gate"
    A rule built into the code, not just a policy: certain actions
    (sending the newsletter, turning on an ad, raising a budget)
    physically cannot happen unless a matching "a human approved
    this" record exists in the database first. This is the main
    safety mechanism of the whole project.


GOALS OF THIS PROJECT
--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Take the repetitive, data-heavy work off a solo operator's plate
   (finding article ideas, drafting issues, generating ad variations,
   crunching CAC/LTV numbers) so one person can run an ad-funded
   newsletter without a team.
2. Never let automation make an irreversible or costly mistake alone
   — every risky action needs a human click.
3. Make the numbers honest and visible at all times — one dashboard
   answering "is this business actually working, and where should
   money go next?"
4. Build it in a way that's been actually tested against real
   services (real database, real email platform, real AI calls) —
   not just code that looks right on paper.


ROADMAP — THE 8 BUILD STEPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------
[DONE] 1. Foundation
        The database structure and the financial ledger. Every other
        step depends on this.

[DONE] 2. Beehiiv integration
        Connects to the real email platform: syncs subscribers,
        sends issues (with the approval gate), tracks open rates.

[DONE] 3. Content sourcing pipeline
        Automatically finds and scores relevant articles/posts every
        6 hours.

[DONE] 4. Draft generator + approval dashboard
        AI writes newsletter drafts; human reviews and approves on a
        web dashboard.

[DONE] 5. Creative Engine + Facebook/Instagram integration
        AI generates ad variations; uploads them to Meta (always
        paused, never auto-activated).

[DONE] 6. Attribution reconciliation
        Cross-checks Facebook's reported signups against real
        confirmed subscribers.

[DONE] 7. The Reaper
        Automatic, no-override kill switch for ads/experiments that
        are losing money or expired.

[DONE] 8. The Allocator + full dashboard
        The recommendation engine (scale up / hold / cut / halt) and
        the complete visual dashboard — flywheel gauge, retention
        curves, creative leaderboard, live experiments list.

All 8 steps are built, tested, and pushed to GitHub. That means the
SOFTWARE is finished. It does NOT mean the BUSINESS is running yet —
see the next section.


WHERE WE ARE RIGHT NOW (BE HONEST WITH YOURSELF ABOUT THIS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
- The code for all 8 steps exists, works, and has been tested — both
  with automated tests (276 tests, all passing) and by actually
  running it against real services (real database, real Beehiiv
  account, real AI API calls). Several real bugs were found and
  fixed this way.

- HOWEVER, the actual newsletter business has NOT launched yet:
    * Real subscribers so far: 1 (a test signup)
    * Newsletter issues actually sent to anyone: 0
    * Real ad campaigns running: 0
    * Real revenue earned: $0
    * Real money spent: about $0.16 (just AI testing costs)

- The impressive-looking dashboard screenshot shown earlier (with
  $28,000+ balance, growing subscriber cohorts, etc.) was generated
  from FAKE, made-up demo data — created specifically to prove the
  dashboard and math work correctly before trusting them with real
  money. It is not real income.

- A few real content items are still placeholders and need a human's
  real input before the business can actually go live:
    * The newsletter's "voice" file still has a placeholder author
      name that an AI mistakenly treated as literal text once.
    * Facebook/Instagram ad account details (Page ID, ad set, target
      link) are still fill-in-the-blank placeholders — no ad account
      has been connected yet.
    * No sponsors have been contacted or signed.


WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY TURN THIS ON (NEXT STEPS TO GO LIVE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Finish the newsletter's voice/style file (fix the placeholder
   author name).
2. Review and send that one drafted issue for real, to build a real
   subscriber base — even without ads yet, e.g. via personal network,
   social posts, etc.
3. Decide on a real offer (free newsletter? paid tier?) and connect a
   real Facebook/Instagram ad account so the Creative Engine has
   somewhere real to push ads.
4. Reach out to real sponsors once there's a real audience size worth
   selling.
5. Let the system run for real for a while, watch the dashboard, and
   start trusting its CAC/LTV/payback numbers only once they're built
   from real subscribers, not the demo data.
